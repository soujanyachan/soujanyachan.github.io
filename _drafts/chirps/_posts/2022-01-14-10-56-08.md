There is a pipeline from a gifted child to burnt out perfectionist adult.

Being gifted at a young age can be more of a disadvantage than a strength. Since school tends to be easy for them,
they do not learn the socialisation, habits and the study methods other kids do.

So a gifted child is special needs in that they require different things to excel.

There is also a burden of expectation on them to live up to their unbridled potential that it constructs a lofty goal in their mind.

Consider two people having the blueprints to a shack and a mansion. The amount of effort it takes to build a mansion is more, the expectation is more. 
It is quicker and easier to build a perfect shack than it is to build an imperfect mansion.
Putting in the same amount of effort into both, which will be further along?

A similar thing happens to gifted kids where the blueprint is the potential and the construction is the current accomplishments.

Shame and low self esteem is the final state that comes from performance falling short of expectations, it compounds and then you get burnt out.
A gifted kid's identity is so wrapped up in being super smart that it's impossible to fail. It's actually going to take a lot of work to achieve potential.

The main thing to do to help is to stop comparison. The word gifted has no meaning outside a comparative framework. Almost all of these expectations are artificial from parent, from society or from teachers.

Gifted kids often take the scenic route through life

You are everything you're capable of in the moment, potential is meaningless.

Another important thing to do is to let go of ego and persevere.

An exercise: The worst experience at a restaurant is to expect really good food and have really bad food. So next time, ask the host to surprise you - practice messing with your expectations .

Since games match the pace of the child and school doesn't that's why video game addiction happens. School moves at the pace of the slowest one and so they are bored. Challenge them more not less and have faith in them. Respect not expect.
