You have never met me.

I have built our entire on onboard process from scratch. It has been refined for 7 years over countless teams and developers and qa. 

1. I built a walkthrough wiki that goes click by click on how to setup your developer machine from the second you log in. 

2. I have a Terms and Definitions wiki that explains every 3 letter acronym and most business terms.

3. I have architecture diagrams that covers all the products and how they interact at a really 1000 foot view. Then you can click on sections to “zoom in” on a specific project and i lt will show the the architecture used for that specific code base, the languages and frameworks and highlights key projects to review, ones with high code churn.

4. My onboarding wiki includes 4 videos each an hour long. These cover(a) all of the stuff in step 3, (B) demos of the products you will be working on (c) explanation of our work process. Also, these videos where made while onboarding other devs so they include what ever question they asked.

5. The onboarding guide includes a list of important likes, such as our environments wiki, agile process, architecture diagrams etc.. All of my wikis are links and organized in a fluid way that lets you navigate easily during the onboarding process.

6. I schedule 2 touch points your first week to cover questions you probably have. Then 3 the second week. The last one I cover specifically the area of code for your first bug/story/feature and our expectations.

7. Last in week 3 I pair you with a long term dev to answer any questions you have about your work.

8. Welcome to the team, let’s get a beer.

*edit* I forgot to mention that your first “story” on week one is to go through all of my onboarding material and update any mistakes or information that has changed. This ensures it keeps up to date.
