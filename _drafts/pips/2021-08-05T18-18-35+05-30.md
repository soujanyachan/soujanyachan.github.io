Honestly? Look for a new job and ask questions during the interview to figure out how the environment is. Ask people how their day to day is structured. How tasks are allocated. A new technology that was added in the last 6 months and how it was added. Talk about how a specific project was conceived and built. And so on. Given the length of interviews nowadays there's enough time across all of them to ask a lot of questions. How much time did they spend during the last month paying off technical debt. As specific questions about specific experience they've had and not generic questions. Do not ask "how is the work life balance" but rather "over the last week when did you roughly start and end work."

I manage data science at a 120 person startup and my team members have around 2 hours of scheduled meetings a week. They tend to schedule a few more ad hoc ones between themselves or other teams but even that is only a couple more hours. Any expense or SaaS product under $1000 a month basically take 15 minutes of time to get approved. We have just enough processes to avoid issues and no more with a periodic review of existing processes. A week ago we agreed to devote a month of team time over the next quarter to pay off technical debt in some of our systems.

edit: If you join a startup make sure your manager chain has actually managed at a previous company and that this isn't their first rodeo. Otherwise you will not have a fun time and they will eventually be replaced by someone who has experience with unknown impact.
