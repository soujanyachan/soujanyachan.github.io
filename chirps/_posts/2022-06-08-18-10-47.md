As a parent who has gone through the same introspection over and over, my favourite conclusion is that the issue is lack of preparation, caused by lack of information and compounded by poorly defined expectations.

Nobody ever tells you, really tells you how hard rearing kids is. They'll all jokingly talk about sleeplessness, poop and pee, and stepping on Lego. Nobody tells you about the irrationality of it all, the mind numbing, never-ending routines, the way the wail of a baby in your ear rends mind processes to shreds, the futility of attempting to get them to do something as basic as falling asleep and the crippling frustration and anger that ensues. More important of all, nobody tells you about the complete erasure of the self, the death of the person you were by any way in which you could have defined it to instead become a permanently stressed automaton working 24/7 the most thankless job there is.

In other words, nobody ever talks about what mothers have gone through for the entirety of time. The biggest difference is that society has always expected women to do this. In fact, they used to be trained for it almost from their own infancy. So they've done this brutally hard task in obscurity and silence, without complaining... Because what's the point of complaining about something so conditioned to be intrinsical to their nature?

That's were the dad rage comes from. For us men, involved parenthood is a choice we "valiantly" take; a magnanimous favour to our overworked partners. As such, the unexpected, brutal reality of it all feels as an unspeakable injustice with no perpetrator. A personal injury that can only be relieved by cathartically blaming it on the little creature at the centre of it all. Rage and its manifestations then feel like well-deserved payback on a tired, frustrated and sleep-deprived mind.

I'm going to raise my sons in the expectations of involved parenthood (and, crucially, on the knowledge that if they feel they're not cut for it, they don't have to have kids and that's okay regardless of the ideas that society pushes on them). It's my hope that it'll spare them if only a bit of this curse of rage.
